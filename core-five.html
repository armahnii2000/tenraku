Falling — Core Five (5)

The five rule-sets chosen as Falling's core, in build order — ranked by how directly light already lives in each one's rules. (Flood, Flip and Shakashaka sit on the reserve bench.) Every board below is a real published example pulled from its original source, not a redrawn board.

1 · Light Up

Board: Simon Tatham's Portable Puzzle Collection (chiark.greenend.org.uk)

Place bulbs so every white cell is lit along its row or column. No bulb may shine on another bulb, and a numbered black wall must have exactly that many bulbs in the cells directly beside it.

In Falling: It already is a light game — bulbs, and walls that block the beams. Falling version: placing scarce light sources in Leo's darkening floor plan.

2 · Black Box

Board: Simon Tatham's Portable Puzzle Collection (chiark.greenend.org.uk)

Balls hide inside a dark box. Fire lasers in from the rim and deduce where they sit: H = hit dead-on, R = reflected straight back out, and a ball at the beam's corner bends it 90°, so it exits somewhere else entirely.

In Falling: Hidden soot motes bend and deflect probe light — you map the dark by how the light behaves. This is already Falling's soot-mote concept.

3 · Masyu (Tatham: Pearl)

Board: Simon Tatham's Portable Puzzle Collection — listed there as “Pearl” (chiark.greenend.org.uk)

Draw one loop through every pearl. A white pearl: the loop goes straight through it, but must turn in at least one cell beside it. A black pearl: the loop turns on it, but must go straight through both cells beside it.

In Falling: The light path itself obeys the pearls: straight through the white ones, turning on the black ones.

4 · Hashiwokakero (Bridges)

Board: Simon Tatham's Portable Puzzle Collection — listed there as “Bridges” (chiark.greenend.org.uk)

Connect the numbered islands with bridges: at most two between any pair, bridges never cross, each island's number equals the bridge-ends touching it, and everything must end up as one connected network.

In Falling: A light/power network — bridges are lit links, each hub needs exactly its number of connections, and the whole grid must hold together as one.

5 · Mid-Loop

Board: SortedPuzzles Mid-Loop example, puzzle MDLO-5-005P (sortedpuzzles.com)

Draw a single loop that passes straight through every dot — and each dot must be the exact midpoint of the straight stretch it sits on: the line runs equally far in both directions from the dot before it may turn.

In Falling: The light loop passes straight through each dot with the dot at the exact midpoint of its straight stretch.
